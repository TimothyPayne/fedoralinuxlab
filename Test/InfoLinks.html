EXPLORE · WORK AND LEARNING

AI, work, and your next skill.

Curious about how work is changing? Start with one example, then look up a job that interests you. You do not need to predict the whole future to choose something useful to learn.

See people and machines working together

  • Automation in restaurants — dated examples and a short imagined shift. Notice what people still check, coordinate, and decide.
  • Factory work, then and now — compare production methods and ask which skills workers can start practicing.

Choose one question: “What task does the machine do, and what does the person need to understand?” A change to one task does not tell us what will happen to every job.

Look up a job and its skills

These links take you to outside websites. Start with one job title rather than a long list of possible careers.

  • My Next Move — search a job title or browse an industry to explore the work, skills, and preparation involved. Try a job you already know something about.
  • New Mexico Department of Workforce Solutions: job-market information — find routes to occupation and wage information, employment data, and career resources. This is more detailed; use it when you have a specific question about New Mexico.

When reading wages or forecasts, check the date, location, and occupation. An average wage is not an offer, and a projection is not a promise. These resources describe careers and job markets; they do not prove that AI will replace a particular job.

Resource pages reviewed 4 October 2026. Check the date on the information you use; reports and figures can change.

Choose one thing to practice.

Think of a task from work, school, or home: explaining a problem, organizing a few steps, or writing a clear message.

You could ask AI: “I want to practice explaining a problem clearly. Give me a simple made-up workplace situation. Let me write a short message, then suggest one improvement. Ask me one question at a time.”

Use made-up details. Read the suggestion and decide whether it makes your meaning clearer. Keep one example of what you learned.
