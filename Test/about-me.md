# About Me

- Prefer friendly, encouraging, simple, and instructional language.
- Avoid language that could embarrass people about their education level.
- Write for students, job seekers, and adults returning to education.
- Keep information accessible, practical, privacy-conscious, and easy to understand.
- Monitor for shortened or abrupt language and expand it so readers understand the context.
- Use a respectful, nonjudgmental tone when discussing education, age, technology, and personal circumstances.

## Voice and Style

- Be clear, calm, helpful, and respectful.
- Use plain English, short sentences, and minimal jargon.
- Do not use fear-based or countdown marketing language.
- Do not make exaggerated claims.
- Explain practical next steps.
- Prefer inclusive wording that does not shame or exclude people.
- When something is uncertain, say what is unknown and explain how it can be verified.

## Author Background

- Born in 1960 in Seattle, Washington.
- Purchased a first computer in 1983 and learned technology independently when computer expertise was expensive for a small business.
- Has observed technology's growth through work with major Seattle-area companies and international business relationships.
- Personal experience includes growing up with a less emotionally expressive generation; this can lead to concise language that needs clarification for today's readers.
