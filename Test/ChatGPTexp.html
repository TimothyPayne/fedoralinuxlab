How AI Figured Out the Answer (and How to Ask Better Questions)

My Question on a movie seen 40 years ago:

"I'm looking for a movie I have seen. I think: 1980s, Australian, set in early 1900s South African Bore war or something, the officer is brought up on war crime charges."

ChatGPT explains how it got the correct answer: Breaker Morant

What Happened in This Example

Your Question (shortened):

1980s movie, Australian, early 1900s South African war, officer on trial for war crimes

Even though some details were a bit off (“Bore war” instead of “Boer War”), the question included multiple strong clues.

Why That Worked

AI doesn’t “remember watching movies.” Instead, it:

  • Looks for patterns in the information you give

  • Matches those patterns to known information

  • Weighs which answer fits most of the clues

In your case, the key clues were:

  • Time period: 1980s

  • Country: Australian

  • Setting: South African war (Boer War)

  • Plot: trial for war crimes

That combination is very specific, and only a small number of films match all of it. One stands out clearly—so the AI can make a confident guess.

What the AI Did Step-by-Step (Simplified)

  1. Pulled out key details
    (1980s, Australian, war, trial)

  2. Matched them to known topics
    → Boer War + Australian soldiers + court-martial

  3. Checked for strong matches
    → One film fits almost perfectly

  4. Gave the best match and explained why
    → So you can confirm or reject it

Why the Answer Included Bullet Points

The explanation listed:

  • Time period

  • Setting

  • Plot

  • Characters

That wasn’t random—it’s meant to help you quickly verify:

“Yes, that’s the one” or “No, that’s not it”

Good AI answers don’t just guess—they justify the guess.

What You Did Right (This Matters)

You gave:

  • A time frame

  • A location

  • A type of story

  • A key event (trial)

That’s exactly what makes AI effective.

Even though:

  • “Bore War” was misspelled

  • Some details were uncertain (“or something”)

You still gave enough signal to outweigh the noise.

What Students Should Learn From This

1. More Clues = Better Answers

Bad:

What’s that movie with a war?

Better:

1980s movie, Australian, war setting, trial for war crimes

2. Imperfect Is Okay—But Be Descriptive

You don’t need to be exact. You do need to be:

  • Specific

  • Descriptive

  • Honest about uncertainty

3. Include What You're Unsure About

Saying:

“or something”

actually helps—it tells the AI:

  • Some details may be fuzzy

  • It should look for close matches, not exact ones

4. Give Context, Not Just Keywords

This worked because you didn’t just say:

Australian war movie

You added:

  • Time period

  • Location

  • Plot detail

That turns a guess into a high-confidence match.

Bottom Line

AI works best when you:

  • Provide multiple specific clues

  • Include context (what it’s about)

  • Don’t worry about being perfect

  • Give enough detail to narrow things down

If you only give one vague detail, you’ll get a vague answer.
If you give a handful of strong clues, you’ll often get the exact answer—even if some details are slightly wrong.
