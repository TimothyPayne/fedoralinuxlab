SEE AN EXAMPLE · EVERYDAY WORDS ARE ENOUGH

What an AI conversation looks like.

You ask for something. The AI replies. If the result is close but not quite right, you tell it what to change.

This short example is adapted from a recorded conversation about this website. The wording is shortened to make it easier to follow.

1. Say what you want

Person: Put these three website links in separate boxes.

AI: I’ll put the links in three boxes and keep the rest of the page as it is.

The AI describes what it plans to do. You can correct it if it misunderstood.

2. Look at the result and ask for a change

Person: This is close, but I wanted narrower, taller boxes.

AI: I’ll make the boxes narrower and taller, keeping the same three links.

You do not need special wording. Tell it which part is useful and which part needs changing.

3. Add another detail

Person: Put a small picture in the top-left corner of the first box, above the text.

AI: I’ll add the picture to the first box and leave the other two boxes as they are.

One small change at a time makes the result easier to check.

You still check the work. In this example, you would look at the page: are there three links, do they work, and did anything else change? An AI saying it made a change does not prove the result is correct.

This conversation used a coding assistant with access to website files. An ordinary AI chat can suggest wording or instructions, but does not automatically have access to change your files.

You can use the same approach.

For a message, a list, or an explanation: ask, read the answer, then describe one change you want. You can ask it to use simpler words or make the answer shorter.
