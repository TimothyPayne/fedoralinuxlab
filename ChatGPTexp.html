SEE AN EXAMPLE · A FEW CLUES CAN HELP

Find a movie from clues.

You remember part of a movie, but not its title. You can ask AI using what you remember, even when the details are a little fuzzy.

1. Describe what you remember

Here is a shortened version of a question from the lab's original example:

I'm looking for a movie I saw years ago. I think it was Australian, from the 1980s, and set during the Boer War in South Africa. An officer is on trial for war crimes. What movie might it be?

The question gives a time period, a country, a setting, and a part of the story. That gives the AI more to work with than “What was that war movie?”

The original question misspelled “Boer War.” You do not need perfect spelling to start. Say “I think” or “I'm not sure” when a detail is uncertain.

2. Treat the answer as a suggestion

In the recorded example, the AI suggested Breaker Morant. Your AI chat may give a different answer.

A confident reply can still be wrong. Ask yourself: does the suggested movie fit the clues, and does its description match what I remember?

3. Check the match

Australia's National Film and Sound Archive describes Breaker Morant as a 1980 Australian film about Australians on trial during the Boer War. That supports the main clues in this example.

Check the film record at the National Film and Sound Archive.

If the answer does not fit your memory, say what is different and add another clue. For example: “I don't remember that scene. Can you suggest another possibility?”

Try it with something you remember.

Think of a movie, book, or song. Describe a few details, mark the ones you are unsure about, and check the suggested answer against another source.

The useful habit is simple: give clues, read the suggestion, and check it. More detail can help, but it does not guarantee the right answer.
